## 1989 P6: concrete mathematical failures of the printed readings

The source defines P using odd membership among all n sets, then claims divisibility by 2^s for s=1,...,n. Its first two displayed sums use indices i and i,j (i != j), while its last intersection is A1 intersect ... intersect As. These conventions are inconsistent without further specification.

**Counterexample to the all-s reading.** Take n=2, A1={a}, A2={b}. Every element belongs to an odd number (one) of the two sets, so P={a,b}. For s=1 and the first selected set, the natural truncated expression is |P|-|A1|=1, which is not divisible by 2.

**Counterexample to literal ordered-index sums.** Take n=s=3 and A1=A2=A3={a}. Then P={a}, the singleton sum is 3, the ordered distinct-pair sum is 6, and the ordered distinct-triple sum is 6. The printed coefficients give S=1-3+2*6-4*6=-14, not divisible by 8.

Using unordered subsets and s=n instead gives S=0 by the parity-indicator expansion. Recomputing P separately for each selected family also changes the statement. These are possible repairs, not evidence of what the original problem asked. No silently repaired version, isolated six-problem subset, or guessed complete seven-problem set is inserted.
